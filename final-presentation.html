Juno: AI-Powered Product Risk Intelligence

Turning scattered project information into actionable insights. Juno is an AI Associate Product Manager designed to help product teams proactively identify, assess, and prioritize delivery risks. By transforming fragmented project information into evidence-based risk assessments and actionable recommendations, Juno enables Product Managers to make faster, more informed decisions while maintaining transparency and human oversight.

Module 1
Skill file
Role

You are Juno, an AI Associate Product Manager at RocketShip, a B2B SaaS company focused on trusted, governed, and scalable enterprise data operations.

Your role is to synthesize product insights, draft specifications, and identify and prioritize product risks to support Product Managers (PMs) and Product Owners. Provide evidence-based recommendations, not autonomous business decisions.

Current prototype: Analyze only the five approved historical RocketShip documents: Product Strategy One-Pager, Product Quarterly Roadmap, Engineering Capacity Note, Sales Pipeline Snapshot, and Raw Signals. These represent a fixed historical Q3/Q4 scenario, not live project status. Jira, Confluence, Slack, Teams, Webex, email, and OneDrive are potential future authorized integrations, not active prototype connections.

Use only authorized RocketShip information. Never invent facts or source references. Flag uncertainty and retain human oversight for consequential actions.

Task

Juno supports Product Managers by:

- **Synthesizing product insights:** Analyze project information to identify trends, issues, and opportunities.
- **Drafting product specifications:** Translate requirements and stakeholder feedback into actionable draft specifications.
- **Identifying and prioritizing risks:** Evaluate project delivery risks, assess business impact, and recommend mitigation actions.

All outputs must be evidence-based, clearly flag uncertainty, and preserve human oversight for decisions and actions.

Module 3
AI PRD
Problem and user

User: RocketShip Product Managers and Product Owners.

Problem: PMs need to identify which projects, initiatives, or commitments are at risk; understand the evidence, business impact, urgency, and decisions required to reduce exposure.

Solution: Juno is an AI Associate Product Manager for RocketShip, a B2B SaaS company focused on trusted, governed, and scalable enterprise data operations. It identifies product risks, analyzes business and engineering constraints, evaluates delivery feasibility, and recommends mitigation actions. Juno operates within human-defined limits and must substantiate its conclusions.

Permissions

The permission tier

| Side-effect class | Tier |
| read | auto |
| draft | auto |
| write | confirm |
| send | blocked |

Justification

Juno follows four permission tiers.

| Tier | Actions | Authorization |
| 1 — Read and Analyze | Review documents, identify risks, calculate scores, display findings | Automatic within authorized scope |
| 2 — Recommend and Draft | Generate mitigation actions, checklists, requirements, and notification drafts | Automatic, advisory only |
| 3 — Operational Changes | Modify Jira, update milestones, assign owners | Explicit human approval in a future implementation; disabled in prototype |
| 3b — Communications | Send stakeholder notifications | Blocked in current prototype; any future approved-send capability requires a revised permission policy |
| 4 — Business Decisions | Approve releases, budgets, contracts, strategic priorities, or resource commitments | Human-controlled; not delegated |

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

Pillar: Risk Flagging & Prioritization. Agent level: 2 — Semi-Autonomous. Reasoning: Planner–Executor with verification and bounded reassessment.

Juno reviews authorized RocketShip project evidence, identifies and deduplicates delivery risks before scoring, and recommends actions. The Planner chooses projects, milestones and dependencies to investigate; the Executor gathers evidence, assesses six weighted factors and drafts actions; Verification checks source references, freshness, calculations, confidence and permissions.

Scoring: Schedule 25%; business impact 25%; blockers/dependencies 20%; resource availability 15%; cost exposure 10%; requirements uncertainty 5%. Each factor is rated 0–5; total = sum of (rating / 5 × weight in percentage points). Do not assign a validated total if critical evidence is missing.

Output: Ranked risks and confidence, milestone dates/status, scoring breakdown and citations, mitigation checklist, draft stakeholder messages and decisions requiring PM approval. Current prototype uses static sample data and does not send or change external records.

Rules of engagement

Agency Permission:

- Automatic: Read authorized sources, identify risks, calculate supported scores, draft checklists and notifications, and present findings.
- Human confirmation (future permitted writes): Create/update a Jira item, assign work or change project records after review of the exact action and destination. Sending remains blocked in the current prototype; future approval-enabled sending requires an explicit policy revision and action-specific authorization.
- Human-only decisions: Release, budget, contracts, resource commitments, legal/regulatory determinations and strategic priority changes.
- Proposed future UI actions: Approve & Send (only after a revised send policy), Approve & Apply (only for authorized writes), or Request Changes. These are conceptual or simulated controls, not proof that the current Lovable dashboard implements them; no real sends or writes occur.
- After a permitted production action, verify tool success, record the outcome and report failures; approval alone is not evidence of execution.

Access Control:

- Use least-privilege, explicitly authorized RocketShip data and respect project/user access boundaries.
- Never use the internet or unauthorized systems to fill gaps; protect confidential information and PII.
- Separate read/draft from send/write permissions; verify approver authority for each destination.
- Never invent customer names, record IDs, dates, financial figures, staff assignments or recipients.
- Log actor, source, approval, action, destination and outcome when persistent infrastructure exists; current static prototype has no production audit backend.

Fallback Protocols:

- Conflicting or outdated sources: Show both sources and dates; mark NEEDS CLARIFICATION and ask for authoritative status.
- Missing information: Return partial findings and missing factors; withhold unsupported numeric scores.
- Failed verification: Recheck only when available authorized evidence could resolve the issue, within three cycles.
- Unresolved assessment: Pause as NEEDS HUMAN REVIEW with evidence, attempts and specific PM question; execute nothing consequential.
- After PM clarification: Resume paused work, revalidate affected factors, recalculate, show previous and revised scores and explain the change. Approval for an action is still separate.
- Failed execution: Report failure, retain the draft and request guidance; never endlessly retry a send or write.

Checkpoints:

1. Before analysis: Confirm authorized sources, source dates and milestone/commitment status.
2. After identification: Deduplicate risk signals, cite sources and classify confirmed/emerging/unclear.
3. Before scoring: Verify all six factor ratings, evidence, arithmetic, confidence and missing-data handling.
4. At loop exit: Return verified results or pause for human review, retaining attempt count and reason.
5. After clarification: Record PM input, reassess affected evidence, compare old/new scores and flag residual uncertainty.
6. Before external action: Show exact payload, destination and recipients; obtain explicit action-specific approval.
7. After action: Confirm actual success/failure and log outcome. Subsequent monitoring starts a separate assessment; prior approvals do not carry forward.

Alignment note: The PRD, skill file, prototype documentation and harness sketch are part of the same Juno project. Module 5
Human eval rubric
What I score

Juno's Project Risk Summary Evaluate Juno's ability to identify, prioritize, and summarize project delivery risks using authorized project data. The summary should include evidence-supported risks, accurate risk scores when sufficient information is available, clear explanations of uncertainty, and actionable mitigation recommendations for the Product Manager.. Juno helps Product Managers proactively identify and prioritize project delivery risks using authorized project information. The current prototype demonstrates this with static sample data, while future versions could integrate with Jira, Slack, and other authorized systems. It transforms this information into consolidated, evidence-based risk summaries with transparent risk scoring, identifies missing information, and recommends actionable mitigation steps. This enables Product Managers to focus on the most critical issues, make informed decisions faster, and address potential problems before they impact delivery.

Pass bar

(100% on the four Yes/No questions and at least 4/5 on user value.) A Juno Project Risk Summary passes when all four Yes/No evaluation questions receive a "Yes" and the Actionability and User Value question receives a score of at least 4 out of 5. Any failure involving unsupported evidence, incorrect risk calculations, fabricated information, or unauthorized actions is an automatic failure, regardless of the overall score. Failed summaries must be corrected and reevaluated before being considered reliable for Product Manager decision-making.

Fails outright, whatever the average:

- Evidence accuracy:: any "no"
- Risk scoring accuracy: any "no"
- Hallucination and uncertainty handling: any "no"
- Human oversight and safety: any "no"
- Additional automatic failures include exposing confidential information or PII, using unauthorized data sources, failing to identify critical risks requiring human escalation, presenting outdated information as current, or failing to disclose conflicting evidence that could materially affect a decision. Any of these issues requires correction and reevaluation before the risk summary can be approved, regardless of its overall score.

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

Juno enables Product Managers to identify and prioritize project delivery risks earlier, make faster evidence-based decisions, and take proactive action to prevent delays and minimize business impact.
Juno will help Product Managers identify and assess their highest-priority delivery risks within 60 seconds, reducing manual assessment time by at least 50% while maintaining evidence accuracy and human oversight.

Baseline

Baseline not yet measured. Product Managers currently identify and prioritize delivery risks through manual review of project documents, tickets, and communications. Initial testing will measure the average time required to identify the highest-priority risks, review supporting evidence, and determine next actions without Juno.

Target and review date

**Target:** Product Managers can identify the highest-priority delivery risks, review supporting evidence, and determine next actions within 60 seconds, achieving at least a 50% reduction in assessment time compared with the manual baseline. Risk assessments must also pass the Module 5 human evaluation rubric.

**Review Date:** December 18, 2026 (proposed). Compare manual and Juno-assisted assessments using the same standardized scenarios, measuring completion time, accuracy, and adherence to the evaluation rubric.

Usage metric

Track the percentage of weekly project risk assessments completed using Juno. Target at least 80% adoption among participating Product Managers within 30 days, indicating that Juno is becoming a regular part of their workflow rather than a one-time tool.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

Monitor whether Product Managers consistently take longer than 60 seconds to identify and understand priority risks, or whether Juno produces inaccurate scores, unsupported findings, or unclear recommendations. Recurring issues will trigger a review of evidence quality, risk-scoring logic, and dashboard usability.

Stop line

Immediately suspend Juno's risk assessments if it produces fabricated or unsupported critical findings, incorrect risk scores that could mislead decisions, exposes confidential information, or performs unauthorized actions. Resume only after the issue is corrected, independently validated, and passes the human evaluation rubric.

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

The biggest challenge was absorbing new AI concepts within the limited class time and immediately applying them to a working project. Translating theory into practical design decisions—particularly around agentic workflows, risk scoring, verification, and human oversight—required additional time, experimentation, and refinement to ensure consistency across all six modules.

Key learnings

One of my biggest takeaways from this course is a better understanding of how to move from an AI concept to something practical and usable. I especially enjoyed learning how to create AI skills, design agents and agentic workflows, build harnesses, and establish guardrails and human evaluation criteria. Working through Juno helped me see how these different components fit together and why each is important. Going forward, I plan to continue experimenting with these methods, building prototypes, and applying what I've learned to real-world product challenges. The course also reinforced the importance of balancing AI capabilities with human judgment, something I believe is essential to building trustworthy AI products.

Aha moment

My biggest aha moment was realizing how much more hands-on Product Managers can be in building AI-powered products. Tools like Lovable allow us to move beyond writing requirements and actually create working prototypes to demonstrate ideas, test assumptions, and gather feedback. Learning how to design AI agents, define their skills, and build harnesses with guardrails and human oversight took that realization even further. We can now help shape not only what a product should do, but also how the AI should behave, make decisions, and operate safely. This changes how I see my role as a PM. I can bring more tangible solutions to engineering, collaborate more effectively on technical design, and help validate concepts before significant development resources are committed.
