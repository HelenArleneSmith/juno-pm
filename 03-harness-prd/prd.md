## Project Scope and Design Evolution

Juno has **three product-management pillars**: signal synthesis, specification drafting, and risk identification/prioritization. This project prototypes **only the third pillar**.

### Risk Identification Process

**Before scoring, Juno must:**

- Extract potential risks from each source.
- Group related signals describing the same underlying problem.
- Cross-reference evidence across the five documents.
- Identify the affected initiative, customer, or business objective.
- Classify the risk as confirmed, emerging, or requiring clarification.
- Identify the relevant RocketShip strategic pillar and priority where supported.
- Evaluate the six scoring factors.
- Juno must not count the same underlying issue as multiple independent risks simply because it appears in several documents.

### Risk Scoring Framework

Calculate an overall risk score from 0–100.

| Risk factor | Weight |
|:--|--:|
| Schedule Risk | 25% |
| Business Impact | 25% |
| Blockers and Dependencies | 20% |
| Resource Availability | 15% |
| Cost Exposure | 10% |
| Requirements Uncertainty | 5% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

Each factor receives a rating from 0–5.

**Formula:**

Risk Score = Σ (Factor Rating ÷ 5 × Factor Weight)

Weights are expressed as percentage points.

#### Factor 1: Schedule Risk — 25%

Definition: The likelihood that an initiative, dependency, or committed deliverable will miss its planned completion date.

**Evaluate:**

- Committed milestone dates.
- Forecast completion dates.
- Work remaining versus available time.
- Delayed or incomplete critical-path activities.
- Dependencies that may prevent timely completion.
- Engineering capacity constraints affecting the timeline.
- Whether delivery dates are committed, proposed, or unknown.

**Scoring criteria:**

| Score | Criteria |
|--:|:--|
| 0 | Evidence confirms delivery is on track |
| 1 | Minor concern with no expected milestone impact |
| 2 | Early warning signs; limited potential delay |
| 3 | Meaningful risk to an upcoming milestone |
| 4 | High likelihood of missing a committed milestone |
| 5 | Confirmed missed critical milestone or evidence that a critical deadline cannot be met |

**RocketShip application:**

SAML/SSO for Okta has an October 1 committed target tied to Acme Corp. Juno should assess schedule risk using the roadmap, engineering capacity, and customer deadline.

The source documents do not establish whether that deadline was ultimately met.

#### Factor 2: Business Impact — 25%

Definition: The potential consequences of a risk for customer trust, revenue, retention, enterprise adoption, or RocketShip's strategic objectives.

**Evaluate:**

- Revenue or renewal exposure documented in Sales.
- Customer retention and expansion consequences.
- Enterprise onboarding blockers.
- Data reliability and customer trust.
- Security, governance, and compliance implications.
- Strategic importance under RocketShip's four pillars.
- Impact on business-critical customer workflows.

**Scoring criteria:**

| Score | Criteria |
|--:|:--|
| 0 | No material business impact supported |
| 1 | Minimal internal or customer inconvenience |
| 2 | Limited business or customer disruption |
| 3 | Meaningful customer, adoption, or operational impact |
| 4 | Significant impact on an important customer, renewal, or strategic objective |
| 5 | Severe documented impact on enterprise trust, business continuity, or a critical contractual/customer commitment |

**RocketShip application:**

Pearson Co has a documented $50,000 ARR renewal flagged at risk following an administrative permissions failure. This supports meaningful business exposure.

Acme Corp has a hard SSO requirement, but no deal value is documented. Juno must not invent one.

Initech has $120,000 ARR, but this is existing account ARR, not the confirmed value of its proposed expansion.

**Deliberate omissions**

### Deliberate Omissions and Rationale

Juno is intentionally designed as an advisory AI agent. The following capabilities are excluded from the prototype:

**1. No autonomous project modifications**

- Juno cannot create, modify, or close Jira tickets, change project timelines, assign resources, or update roadmap priorities.
- **Why:** Risk assessments may be based on incomplete or conflicting information. Product Managers must validate recommendations before changes are made.

**2. No automatic stakeholder notifications**

- Juno can identify stakeholders and draft emails, Slack messages, or Teams notifications but cannot send them.
- **Why:** Unverified risks could create unnecessary escalations or communicate inaccurate information to customers or leadership. A Product Manager must approve communications.

**3. No autonomous business decisions**

- Juno cannot approve budgets, change strategic priorities, commit delivery dates, or authorize product releases.
- **Why:** These decisions require business judgment, organizational accountability, and human oversight.

**4. No unrestricted external information access**

- Juno cannot search the public internet or access unauthorized systems to fill information gaps.
- **Why:** The prototype must produce traceable, evidence-based assessments using only the five approved RocketShip documents.

**5. No automatic risk-score changes based on checklist completion**

- Completing a mitigation task does not automatically lower a project's risk score.
- **Why:** A completed action does not necessarily mean the underlying risk has been resolved. Juno must reassess the supporting evidence before changing a score.
- These omissions are intentional product safeguards that prioritize accuracy, transparency, and human decision-making over autonomous execution.

## 03 Loop · AI Costs & Latency

**Turn ceiling and escalation**

Juno uses a bounded assessment loop.

### Assessment Sequence

#### Step 1 — Gather Evidence

Retrieve relevant information from the five approved RocketShip documents.

#### Step 2 — Identify Risks

Detect issues involving delivery, technical dependencies, customer commitments, staffing, requirements, and business outcomes.

#### Step 3 — Cross-Reference Sources

Determine whether different documents support, contradict, or clarify the same risk.

#### Step 4 — Assess Risk

Evaluate the six weighted factors and determine which ratings have sufficient evidence.

#### Step 5 — Verify

Check source references, calculations, confidence levels, and permissions.

#### Step 6 — Return Results

Display validated findings, partial assessments, or `NEEDS CLARIFICATION`.

### Proposed Planner–Executor–Verification Architecture

1. **Planner:** Choose authorized initiatives, milestones, dependencies and evidence gaps to inspect.
2. **Executor:** Gather and reconcile authorized evidence; deduplicate risks; evaluate six scoring factors; prepare mitigations and drafts.
3. **Verification:** Check source traceability and dates, contradictory claims, factor completeness, deterministic arithmetic, confidence and permissions.

The controller tracks cycle count and meaningful progress. If verification succeeds, return validated findings immediately. If unresolved after three cycles or two consecutive no-progress cycles, preserve verified partial results, pause as `NEEDS HUMAN REVIEW`, and ask the PM a concrete question. Prohibited access or actions cause immediate stop. After PM clarification, preserve prior evidence and cycle history, revalidate the affected assessment, and show the previous versus revised findings where supported. Clarification is **not** approval to send or write. A new cycle budget requires a separately PM-authorized new assessment.

**Prototype limitation:** This controller is a proposed design; the Lovable dashboard does not implement agentic execution, automatic reassessment, or live integrations.

### Execution Limits

Maximum **three** Planner–Executor–Verification cycles per assessment, enforced by a future workflow controller. One initial cycle and up to two corrective cycles are allowed only when new evidence or a specific corrective hypothesis can make progress. Stop earlier after successful verification or two consecutive cycles without meaningful progress.

Retry only if a correctable issue can be resolved using existing authorized evidence.

Do not repeatedly analyze unchanged evidence without a specific reason.

Stop when required information is unavailable or human approval is necessary.

Escalate critical findings immediately.

### Stop Conditions

**Juno stops when:**

- The assessment passes verification.
- Required information cannot be found.
- Evidence remains contradictory.
- Three assessment cycles have been completed, or two consecutive cycles produced no meaningful progress.
- An action requires human approval.
- A request exceeds authorized permissions.

### Human Handoff

**When stopping because of uncertainty, Juno must identify:**

- The affected risk.
- The missing or conflicting information.
- Why the uncertainty matters.
- The appropriate stakeholder role.
- The recommended next action.

**Latency and cost target**

### Latency and Cost Targets

**Response Time Targets — Proposed, Not Yet Measured**

- A future implemented Juno should return the Portfolio Overview in under 3 seconds for at least 95% of requests when using preloaded prototype data.
- Future implemented individual project risk assessments should complete in under 8 seconds for at least 95% of requests.
- Future complex assessments requiring cross-document verification should target completion within 12 seconds.
- If processing exceeds the expected response time, display a loading indicator and return a clear error or partial assessment rather than leave the user waiting indefinitely.

**Cost and Processing Controls**

- Limit each assessment to three Planner–Executor–Verification cycles maximum, stopping earlier on success or two consecutive no-progress cycles.
- Retrieve and analyze only the information relevant to the selected project or risk.
- Reuse previously verified source information when it has not changed.
- Avoid reprocessing all five documents for every dropdown selection or dashboard interaction.
- Calculate weighted risk scores using deterministic formulas rather than additional AI calls.
- Use AI primarily for interpreting evidence, identifying risks, and generating recommendations.
- Keep outputs concise and structured to minimize unnecessary token usage.

**Loop Efficiency**

- Use a single assessment pass whenever sufficient evidence is available.
- Perform a corrective cycle only when verification identifies a resolvable issue and authorized evidence or a specific corrective hypothesis may change the result.
- Do not create nested retry loops or repeatedly call tools that return the same information.
- Stop processing when required evidence is unavailable and display `NEEDS CLARIFICATION`.
- Prioritize fast delivery of the Portfolio Overview, followed by detailed analysis when a project is selected.

**Cost Predictability**

- Keep the number of AI calls per assessment bounded and measurable.
- Track response time, number of assessment attempts, and token usage when supported by the implementation.
- Prevent repeated dashboard interactions from triggering unnecessary AI calls.
- If the cost or processing limit is reached, stop additional analysis and return the available verified findings.
- These are proposed prototype performance targets and must be measured during testing rather than assumed to be achieved.

### Expiry and Write Rules

**Information Expiry**

Juno must distinguish between permanent reference information, current project information, and temporary AI-generated assessments.

- **Product strategy and scoring methodology:** Remain valid until an authorized Product Manager or administrator approves an updated version.
- **Project timelines, milestones, and delivery status:** Consider stale after 24 hours without a successful source refresh in a future production implementation.
- **Risk assessments and scores:** Reassess when relevant source information changes or after 24 hours, whichever occurs first.
- **Engineering capacity and resource availability:** Consider stale after 24 hours because staffing and allocations may change.
- **Customer escalations and critical blockers:** Revalidate within 5 minutes of detecting a relevant source change.
- **Temporary session information:** Retain only during the active session unless explicitly saved through an authorized mechanism.
- **Historical records:** Preserve for reference according to approved retention policies, but never treat them as current without revalidation.
- These are proposed freshness thresholds, not existing RocketShip policies.

**Prototype Expiry Rules**

- The five RocketShip documents remain the fixed historical source of truth for the prototype.
- Source information does not automatically expire or update because no live integrations are enabled.
- Risk assessments remain tied to the version of the documents used to generate them.
- If the prototype data changes, Juno must reassess affected risks before presenting updated conclusions.
- Do not invent newer project statuses or assume historical deadlines have been met.

**Write and Correction Rules**

Juno follows this authority hierarchy:

1. **Authorized human-approved corrections and decisions**, documented in the appropriate system of record.

2. **Current approved source records**, such as the product roadmap, engineering capacity plans, or approved requirements.

3. **Other authorized supporting evidence**, including meeting transcripts, Slack discussions, and emails.

4. **Previously generated Juno assessments**, which are advisory and never override verified source information.

When an authorized human corrects Juno:

- Accept the correction as a proposed update and identify the supporting evidence.
- If the correction is approved and recorded in the authoritative source, use it in subsequent assessments.
- Recalculate affected risk factors and scores.
- Preserve the previous assessment as historical information when retention is supported.
- Record who approved the change and when, if the system supports an audit history.
- Do not overwrite original source documents or silently modify historical evidence.
- If a correction conflicts with another authoritative record, flag `NEEDS CLARIFICATION` until the appropriate owner resolves the conflict.

**Write Permissions**

- Juno may update its temporary analysis and dashboard presentation automatically.
- Juno may not independently change roadmap dates, project status, engineering assignments, budgets, or approved requirements.
- Only authorized humans may approve changes to source records.
- Completing a mitigation checklist does not automatically change a risk score or project status.
- Juno must reassess the underlying evidence before displaying a revised risk assessment.

**Core Principle**

Human-approved source records outrank stored AI conclusions. Juno must never allow outdated assessments or generated assumptions to override verified project information.

2. Add this to Section 5 — Permissions

Permission Tiers, Blast Radius, and Recoverability

Juno uses risk-based permissions determined by the potential impact (blast radius) of an action and whether that action can be reversed.

READ — AUTO

- Juno may automatically read and analyze approved documents, project information, milestones, and risk evidence.
- Blast radius: Low. Reading authorized information does not modify source records.
- Recoverability: No data changes to reverse.
- Mitigation: Restrict access to approved sources and enforce existing user permissions.
- DRAFT — AUTO
- Juno may automatically generate risk assessments, mitigation recommendations, project summaries, and draft stakeholder notifications.
- Blast radius: Low. Drafts remain internal and do not create external commitments.
- Recoverability: High. Drafts can be edited or discarded.
- Mitigation: Clearly label AI-generated content as drafts, include supporting evidence, and prevent automatic distribution.
- WRITE — CONFIRM
- In a future implementation, Juno may propose updates to project status, Jira tickets, milestones, or mitigation tasks, but execution requires explicit approval from an authorized user.
- Blast radius: Moderate to high. Incorrect changes could affect project tracking, resource allocation, or management decisions.
- Recoverability: Partial. Some changes can be reversed, but downstream decisions based on incorrect information may not be.
- Mitigation: Require human approval, show the proposed change before execution, enforce role-based permissions, and maintain an audit history.
- Prototype limitation: External system writes are disabled.
- SEND — BLOCKED
- Juno may draft stakeholder communications but cannot independently send emails, Slack messages, Teams notifications, or customer communications.
- Blast radius: High. Incorrect messages could cause unnecessary escalations, confusion, or unauthorized commitments.
- Recoverability: Low. Messages may be forwarded, copied, or acted upon even after correction.
- Mitigation: Block sending in the prototype. Communications must be reviewed and sent manually by an authorized human.

### Additional AI Risk Mitigations

- Hallucinations: Require source citations and label unsupported claims `NEEDS CLARIFICATION`.
- Outdated information: Display source timestamps and flag stale assessments.
- Incorrect risk scores: Verify calculations against the approved weighted scoring formula.
- Unauthorized access: Limit Juno to approved documents and user-authorized systems.
- Automation errors: Limit assessment retries and prevent autonomous project modifications.
- Human accountability: Keep final project, resource, financial, and communication decisions with authorized personnel.
- Core Principle: Juno should automatically perform low-risk, reversible tasks while restricting actions that could create significant operational or business consequences.

## 06 Verification · AI Testing & Measurement

**The check before output ships**

Juno must verify its analysis before presenting a result as validated.

#### Verification Check 1 — Evidence Traceability

**Every material risk finding must include:**

- Source document.
- Relevant section, record, or excerpt.
- Supporting evidence.
- Source date, if available.
- Juno must not invent Jira IDs or links that do not exist in the supplied documents.

#### Verification Check 2 — Cross-Source Consistency

**Juno must identify:**

- Conflicting project statuses.
- Contradictory commitments.
- Unapproved feature promises.
- Technical dependencies not reflected in proposed delivery plans.
- Differences between executive requests and documented roadmap priorities.
- When sources disagree, Juno must show the disagreement rather than silently select one interpretation.

#### Verification Check 3 — Risk Scoring

**Before displaying a validated numeric score:**

- Confirm all six factors have sufficient evidence.
- Confirm every rating follows the defined criteria.
- Confirm the factor weights total 100%.
- Confirm the weighted calculation is correct.
- Confirm missing data has not been assigned zero.
- Confirm the rationale explains the rating.

#### Verification Check 4 — Strategic Alignment

Verify that proposed priorities align with RocketShip's documented strategy.

**Distinguish:**

- Trust, reliability, and compliance.
- Enterprise adoption and time-to-value.
- Platform leverage and operational scale.
- Incremental or cosmetic improvements.
- Do not equate an executive request with an approved strategic priority.

#### Verification Check 5 — Confidence

Assign confidence to findings and ratings.

High: Strong, consistent, directly supported evidence.

Medium: Reasonable supporting evidence with some limitations.

Low: Limited, indirect, incomplete, or conflicting evidence.

Confidence measures evidence quality, not risk severity.

#### Verification Check 6 — Permissions

**Confirm that:**

- Only authorized information was used.
- No source records were changed.
- No external messages were sent.
- No unapproved commitments were created.
- Human decisions remain clearly identified.

### Verification Failure

**If a check fails:**

- Identify the failed check.
- Retry only when a specific correctable verification issue can be resolved using authorized evidence, within the three-cycle limit; stop after two consecutive no-progress cycles.
- Withhold unsupported numeric scores or conclusions.
- Mark the affected assessment `NEEDS CLARIFICATION`.
- Identify missing evidence or conflicting information.
- Recommend a human review action.

### Final Operating Principle

Juno must prioritize evidence over assumptions, business outcomes over feature volume, and human oversight over autonomous execution.

A risk score is useful only when the underlying evidence, scoring rationale, and limitations are transparent.

### Failure Behavior and Recovery

When verification fails, Juno must clearly communicate the issue, preserve any verified findings, and provide an actionable next step rather than silently withholding the output.

**1. Missing or Insufficient Evidence**

- Display **`NEEDS CLARIFICATION`** for any conclusion or risk score that cannot be supported.
- Explain which information is missing and identify the source or person needed to resolve it.
- Continue displaying verified project information without inventing missing details.

**2. Conflicting Information**

- Flag the conflicting sources and explain the discrepancy.
- Do not automatically assume that the newest information is correct.
- Withhold the affected conclusion until an authorized Product Manager resolves the conflict.
- Continue presenting unaffected findings.

**3. Incorrect Risk Calculations**

- Recalculate the weighted risk score using the approved formula.
- Allow corrective cycles only within the three-cycle limit, with an early stop after two consecutive cycles without meaningful progress.
- If verification still fails, display **`SCORE UNAVAILABLE`** and explain why.
- Never display an unverified numerical score.

**4. Unavailable or Outdated Sources**

- Display **`SOURCE UNAVAILABLE`** or **`STALE DATA`**, including the last successful retrieval date when known.
- Use historical information only when clearly labeled as outdated.
- Do not claim that a project is on track based solely on outdated information.
- Recommend source verification before making consequential decisions.

**5. Unsupported Recommendations**

- Separate verified facts from AI-generated interpretations.
- Remove or label recommendations that lack sufficient supporting evidence.
- Explain what additional information is needed to validate the recommendation.

**6. Permission or Access Failures**

- Display **`ACTION BLOCKED`** and explain which permission is required.
- Do not bypass access restrictions or attempt unauthorized alternatives.
- Provide a draft or manual next step when appropriate.

**7. System Errors or Timeouts**

- Display a clear error message rather than a blank dashboard or misleading result.
- Preserve previously verified results, clearly labeled with their assessment timestamp.
- Offer the user an option to retry the assessment.
- Do not repeatedly retry beyond the defined processing limits.

### Failure Response Format

When Juno cannot complete a verification, it should display:

- **Status:** `NEEDS CLARIFICATION`, `SCORE UNAVAILABLE`, `SOURCE UNAVAILABLE`, or `ACTION BLOCKED`.
- **What Juno knows:** Verified facts with source references.
- **What failed:** The specific verification issue.
- **Impact:** Which project status, risk score, or recommendation is affected.
- **Next action:** What the Product Manager should verify, correct, or approve.

**Core Principle:** Juno prioritizes accuracy, transparency, and actionable partial results over confident but unsupported answers. Verification failures must always be visible to the user.

## Eval plan

*Proposed evaluation plan for Module 6; thresholds and results require human-reviewed testing and have not yet been demonstrated.*

Juno will be evaluated using a predefined set of test scenarios to measure accuracy, reliability, safety, and performance.

### Golden Test Set

Create an initial set of 20 test cases using the five approved RocketShip documents, covering:

- **Risk identification:** Reporting export failures, Acme SSO delivery dependencies, Pearson renewal risk, and AI prototype feasibility.
- **Risk scoring:** Correct application of the six weighted scoring factors, including handling missing evidence.
- **Source accuracy:** Every material finding must reference supporting evidence.
- **Portfolio completeness:** All roadmap initiatives appear, including those without identified risks.
- **Failure handling:** Missing, conflicting, stale, or unavailable information produces a clear explanation and appropriate status.
- **Permission enforcement:** Reading and drafting are automatic; external writes require approval in future implementations; sending remains blocked.
- Each test will define the input, expected output, supporting source evidence, and pass/fail criteria. Expected results will be reviewed and approved by a human evaluator rather than generated solely by Juno.

### Proposed Pass Thresholds

- **Source accuracy:** 100% of material factual claims must have valid supporting references.
- **Risk scoring:** 100% mathematical accuracy when sufficient evidence exists.
- **Unsupported conclusions:** Zero fabricated project statuses, deadlines, financial values, or risk scores.
- **Portfolio completeness:** 100% of documented roadmap initiatives represented.
- **Permission compliance:** 100% adherence to defined action restrictions.
- **Risk identification:** At least 90% recall against the human-reviewed golden test set.
- **Response performance:** At least 95% of standard assessments complete within 8 seconds.
- Any unsupported high-impact claim, unauthorized action, or incorrect risk calculation is a critical failure requiring correction before release.

### Regression Testing Cadence

- Run the golden test set after changes to Juno's prompts, scoring logic, permissions, or source-processing behavior.
- Run regression tests whenever the approved prototype documents are updated.
- Reevaluate failed cases after corrections to ensure previously working behaviors remain intact.
- Review and expand the golden set during Module 6 and before a future source-grounded prototype demonstration.

### Evaluation Outputs

Record the test case, expected result, actual result, pass/fail status, failure reason, and corrective action.

**Core Principle:** Juno must demonstrate evidence-based accuracy, predictable behavior, and permission compliance before its recommendations can be considered reliable.

## Out of scope

Any capability not in the verb list above, and any action tiered `blocked`. Both are decisions on the record, not omissions.

Juno is an AI-powered product management decision-support tool, not an autonomous project manager. The following capabilities are intentionally excluded:

- **Autonomous project changes:** Creating or modifying Jira tickets, delivery dates, roadmaps, resource assignments, or budgets without human approval.
- **Sending communications:** Automatically sending emails, Slack or Teams messages, customer notifications, or stakeholder escalations.
- **Final business decisions:** Approving budgets, changing strategic priorities, committing delivery dates, or authorizing releases.
- **Unauthorized data access:** Accessing external websites, systems, or information beyond approved sources.
- **Unsupported predictions:** Generating risk scores, financial estimates, or project statuses without sufficient verified evidence.
- **Persistent production integrations:** Live system updates, cross-session memory, and automated workflows are excluded from the current prototype.

**Rationale:** These exclusions limit operational risk, prevent unauthorized actions, preserve human accountability, and ensure Juno provides transparent, evidence-based recommendations. Future capabilities may be introduced only after appropriate permissions, verification, and governance controls are established.
